Question 1: Preserving an axis (1 point(s))

An array units has shape (3, 4), with stores in rows and products in columns. What are the shapes of units[:, 1] and units[:, 1:2], respectively?

A. (4,) and (4, 1)

B. (3,) and (3, 1) (Correct answer)

C. (3, 1) and (3,)

D. (1, 4) and (3, 4)

Rationale: An integer column index removes that axis. A one-column slice preserves its length-one axis; both retain all three stores.

Question 2: One multiplier per store (1 point(s))

A revenue array has shape (3, 4). A vector factors has shape (3,), containing one multiplier for each store (row). Which expression applies the correct factor to every product in each store?

A. revenue * factors

B. revenue * factors[None, :]

C. revenue * factors[:, None] (Correct answer)

D. revenue * factors.reshape(1, 3)

Rationale: A (3, 1) factor array matches the three store rows and broadcasts along the four product columns. The other expressions have incompatible trailing sizes 4 and 3.

Question 3: Interpreting totals (1 point(s))

A revenue array has shape (3, 4), with stores in rows and products in columns. What does revenue.sum(axis=1) return?

A. A (3,) vector with one total per store. (Correct answer)

B. A (4,) vector with one total per product.

C. One scalar total across every entry.

D. A (3, 4) array of cumulative totals.

Rationale: Summing along axis 1 removes the product axis and retains one total for each of the three stores.

Question 4: Values, positions, and ties (1 point(s))

For revenue = np.array([[60., 40.], [60., 30.]]), what do revenue.max() and np.argmax(revenue) return, and how is the tie handled?

A. Both return 60; neither can identify a position.

B. The maximum is 60 and argmax is 2, because the last tied occurrence wins.

C. The maximum is 60 and argmax returns both coordinates (0, 0) and (1, 0).

D. The maximum is 60 and argmax is 0, the first position in row-major flattened order. (Correct answer)

Rationale: max returns the value. With no axis, argmax returns the first flattened position at the maximum. argwhere(revenue == revenue.max()) can identify all tied coordinates.
